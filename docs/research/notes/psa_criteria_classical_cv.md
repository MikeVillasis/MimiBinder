# PSA Grading Criteria and Classical (Non-Deep-Learning) CV Techniques for Measuring Them from Phone Photos of Pokemon TCG Cards

Research date: 2026-11-21. Note: psacard.com returned HTTP 403 to the fetcher, and web.archive.org could not be fetched, so the PSA figures below come from secondary sources that quote PSA's pages. Before treating any number as authoritative, check it against https://www.psacard.com/gradingstandards in a browser.

## 1. PSA official grade definitions, centering tolerances, half grades, qualifiers, and recent changes

### Takeaway
As of 2026, PSA's centering limits (front/back) are commonly quoted as: 10 at ~55/45 and 75/25; 9 at ~60/40 and 90/10; 8 at ~65/35 and 90/10; 7 at ~70/30 and 90/10; 6 at 80/20 and 90/10; 5 and 4 at 85/15 and 90/10; 3 through 1.5 at 90/10 and 90/10. PSA cards grading 7 or better get 5% leeway on front centering for eye appeal. Half grades run from 1.5 to 8.5, and there is no PSA 9.5. The one notable recent change is a January 2025 rewording of the PSA 10 front standard to "approximately 55/45" with the 60/40 range dropped. PSA's CEO described it as a wording clean-up, not a change in practice.

### Cited Findings
**Centering per grade (front / back)**
- PSA 10 ~55/45 / 75/25; 9 ~60/40 / 90/10; 8 ~65/35 / 90/10; 7 ~70/30 / 90/10; 6 80/20 / 90/10; 5 85/15 / 90/10; 4 85/15 / 90/10; 3 90/10 / 90/10; 2 90/10 / 90/10; 1.5 ~90/10 / ~90/10; 1 has no figure. PSA has no Pristine tier above 10. The article says it was verified in August 2026. — [CardCenteringCalculator: tolerances by grade](https://www.cardcenteringcalculator.com/articles/card-centering-tolerance-by-grade)
- The same table appears on a guide updated September 2026, which says PSA 1 has no centering limit. — [Rorz Cards PSA scale](https://www.rorzcards.com/the-psa-grading-scale-explained-everything-you-need-to-know/)
- PSA's page states the PSA 10 image must be centered "not to exceed approximately 55/45 percent on the front, and 75/25 percent on the reverse." A December 2023 archived copy reportedly gave ranges instead: 10 = 55/45–60/40, 9 = 60/40–65/35, 8 = 65/35–70/30, 7 = 70/30–75/25. — [psacard.com grading standards (via search snippet)](https://www.psacard.com/gradingstandards); [Elite Fourum: PSA 10 and centering tolerances](https://www.elitefourum.com/t/psa-10-and-centering-tolerances/30614.md)
- **5% leeway:** PSA gives a 5% leeway on front centering to cards grading NM 7 or better, tied to eye appeal. Example: a card meeting every other criterion for a 9 passes automatically at 60/40 and may still pass at 65/35 with good eye appeal. — [Rorz Cards](https://www.rorzcards.com/the-psa-grading-scale-explained-everything-you-need-to-know/); [CardCenteringCalculator](https://www.cardcenteringcalculator.com/articles/card-centering-tolerance-by-grade)
- **How PSA reads centering:** use the worst ratio, at the most off-center part of the card. — [CardCenteringCalculator](https://www.cardcenteringcalculator.com/articles/card-centering-tolerance-by-grade)

**Grade definitions (secondary summary of PSA wording)**
- 10 Gem Mint: sharp corners, full original gloss, no staining, sharp focus. A slight printing imperfection is allowed if it doesn't hurt eye appeal.
- 9 Mint: exactly one minor flaw, such as a slight wax stain on the back, a minor printing imperfection, or slightly off-white borders.
- 8 NM-MT: looks Mint at first glance, but may show a back wax stain, slight fraying on one or two corners, a minor print imperfection, or slightly off-white borders.
- 7 NM: slight surface wear visible on close inspection, slight corner fraying, possibly slightly soft focus; most gloss retained.
- 6 EX-MT: visible wear or a print defect that doesn't spoil appeal; a light scratch, graduated corner fraying, some loss of gloss, very slight notching on the edges.
- 5 EX: slight rounding of the corners, more visible surface wear, minor chipping on the edges, several light scratches.
- 4 VG-EX: slightly rounded corners, modest wear, light scuffing. **This is the highest grade that allows a light crease.**
- 3 VG: rounded corners, noticeable edge wear, most gloss gone, yellowed borders, a visible crease.
- 2 Good: accelerated corner rounding, obvious wear, scratches, staining or chipping, possibly several creases.
- 1.5 Fair: extreme wear, possibly heavy creases; the card must be intact.
- 1 Poor: may be missing one or two small pieces, or too dirty or discolored to identify easily.
- Source for all of the above: [Rorz Cards](https://www.rorzcards.com/the-psa-grading-scale-explained-everything-you-need-to-know/)

**Half grades**
- Half grades are 2.5, 3.5, 4.5, 5.5, 6.5, 7.5 and 8.5, given to cards at the high end of a grade. Centering is the main factor in deciding them. **There is no PSA 9.5.** PSA 1.5 (Fair) is its own grade. — [Rorz Cards](https://www.rorzcards.com/the-psa-grading-scale-explained-everything-you-need-to-know/); [CardCenteringCalculator](https://www.cardcenteringcalculator.com/articles/card-centering-tolerance-by-grade)
- No centering figures are published for half grades, only "half-point" guidance. — [CardCenteringCalculator](https://www.cardcenteringcalculator.com/articles/card-centering-tolerance-by-grade)

**Qualifiers**
- A qualifier means the card meets the grade in every respect except one. Example: "PSA 8 OC" is an 8 that is off center.
- OC (off-center), ST (staining), PD (print defect) and OF (out of focus) are optional. PSA usually lowers the numeric grade instead of applying them.
- MK (marks, e.g. ink or pencil) and MC (miscut) are always applied.
- Qualified cards often sell at about the price of a card two grades lower. This is anecdotal.
- Source for qualifiers: [Rorz Cards](https://www.rorzcards.com/the-psa-grading-scale-explained-everything-you-need-to-know/)

**Recent changes (2024–2026)**
- In January 2025, PSA changed the PSA 10 centering wording on its website without announcing it. CEO Nat Turner, replying on Elite Fourum: "No change in day to day. Just updated the language on the website… That is how we have been operating for a very long time." PSA later acknowledged on X that it had used 55/45 for some time. — [Cardlines](https://cardlines.com/psa-centering-standards-change/)
- **Conflict:** the Cardlines article appears to mislabel which wording is old and which is new. It quotes "55/45 to 60/40" as the new text, while other sources and an Elite Fourum collector say the 60/40 mention was *removed*. The weight of evidence: the range was the older wording, and the current page says "approximately 55/45." — [Cardlines](https://cardlines.com/psa-centering-standards-change/); [Rorz Cards](https://www.rorzcards.com/the-psa-grading-scale-explained-everything-you-need-to-know/); [CardCenteringCalculator](https://www.cardcenteringcalculator.com/articles/card-centering-tolerance-by-grade)
- The guide updated September 2026 reports no further grading-standard changes in 2026. Per GemRate data it cites, PSA's 10 rate fell from 45% (2024) to 43% (H1 2025). — [Rorz Cards](https://www.rorzcards.com/the-psa-grading-scale-explained-everything-you-need-to-know/)

**Other graders' centering (brief)**
- BGS: Pristine 10 / Black Label 50/50 front, 55/45 back. 9.5 is 55/45 both axes front, 60/40 back. 9 is 55/45 front, 70/30 back. 8 is 60/40 front, 80/20 back. BGS applies ratios to each axis independently. — [CardCenteringCalculator](https://www.cardcenteringcalculator.com/articles/card-centering-tolerance-by-grade)
- CGC: Pristine 10 is 50/50 front. Gem Mint 10 is ~55/45 front, 75/25 back. 9 is 60/40 front, 90/10 back. - SGC publishes front limits only (10 Gem 55/45, 9 60/40). — [CardCenteringCalculator](https://www.cardcenteringcalculator.com/articles/card-centering-tolerance-by-grade)

### Inferences
- A predictor should treat PSA 10 centering as a hard gate: max(front L/R, front T/B) ≤ ~55, plus a back gate of ~75. Since the 5% leeway is discretionary and tied to eye appeal, the result should be a probability, not a pass/fail, for ratios between 55 and 60.
- PSA has no published numeric thresholds for corners, edges or surface. The CV metrics have to be calibrated against graded-card data, not derived from the standards.
- Of the qualifiers, OC and MC can be measured geometrically. MK and ST are color anomalies, PD is a print-defect anomaly, and OF is a focus/sharpness measure of the print (e.g. Laplacian variance relative to a reference). OF and PD are hard to separate from blur in the phone photo itself.

- PSA's page could not be fetched directly (403), so no verbatim quotes of PSA's current grade text.
- The exact wording PSA posted on X about 55/45 was not found.
- No source confirms whether any 2026 changes were made after September 2026.

## 2. How centering is measured and how to compute it from an image

### Takeaway
Centering = for each axis, larger border / (sum of both borders), written as e.g. 55/45. It is measured from the cut edge to the inner printed frame, on front and back, and the **worst** reading is used. From an image: warp the card to a canonical rectangle, then measure the four border widths at many points along each side and keep the worst pair. Using the median lets tilted (diamond-cut) frames slip through. At typical phone resolution after the warp (~0.087 mm/px), a 1 px error moves the ratio by about 0.9–1.8 points, which matters because the 55/45 vs 60/40 boundary is only ~0.24 mm on a 2.4 mm border.

### Cited Findings
- Measure left/right and top/bottom on front and back, from the card edge to the outer printed border, and use the worst ratio on each side. PSA describes this as the most off-center part of the card. — [CardCenteringCalculator](https://www.cardcenteringcalculator.com/articles/card-centering-tolerance-by-grade)
- Formula used by collectors: left / (left + right) × 100. Measure at several points and use the worst reading, because cuts can be uneven. — [blakecrosley.com](https://blakecrosley.com/blog/how-card-centering-is-judged) (summarizing PreGradeCards and zero-pop guides)
- **Kiradex app's border finder** (as read from its code by the article author):
  - Rectangle detection, then the corners are pushed outward by a 5% margin so the true cut edge is captured, then a perspective warp to 800 px wide.
  - 31 scan lines per side across the middle 30–70% of the side, avoiding the corners.
  - A boundary is the first pixel where 3 consecutive pixels each differ from the previous color by more than 38 (RGB Euclidean, max 441).
  - Border color is sampled just inside the cut edge, avoiding the shadow.
  - An edge is kept if ≥3 of 5 walks agree within 50%. A border is kept if readings agree within 15%. The median is reported.
  - Sanity bound: a border must be 1.2–12% of the side length.
  - Results are whole pixels, with no sub-pixel step.
  - Source for the Kiradex details: [blakecrosley.com](https://blakecrosley.com/blog/how-card-centering-is-judged)
- **Median vs worst:** in the article's example, a tilted frame reads 50/50 by median, 54/46 by worst walk, and 60/40 at the worst point. The middle-only walks cannot see the ends of the card. — [blakecrosley.com](https://blakecrosley.com/blog/how-card-centering-is-judged)
- **Error arithmetic:**
  - Resolution: the warped card is ~727 px across 63 mm, so ~0.087 mm/px.
  - On a 2.4 mm border, 55/45 vs 60/40 is a difference of 0.24 mm.
  - A 1 px error on the warped image moves the ratio ~0.9 points (one side) or ~1.8 points (both sides in opposite directions).
  - On a 600 dpi scan, 1 px moves it 0.44 / 0.88 points; at 1200 dpi, 0.22 / 0.44.
  - A 0.5 mm ruler gives 4.7–10.4 points of error.
  - The app's own comment claims phone readings are "good to about a point," but this is unmeasured.
  - The article's proposed validation: 10 cards with scan-measured borders × 5 shots each. A worst error ≤1.0 point allows whole-number ratios to be shown.
  - Source for the error arithmetic: [blakecrosley.com](https://blakecrosley.com/blog/how-card-centering-is-judged)
- A photo-based grader (cardgrade.io's DEV post):
  - Detects both the card's outer quad and the inner edge of the printed border.
  - Warps each side onto a flat canvas using bilinear interpolation, replicated borders, and a small background margin for edge contrast.
  - Averages L/R and T/B widths and converts to mm using 63.5 × 88.9 mm.
  - Orders corners by min/max of x+y and x−y.
  - Learned that upscaling small uploads to ~4000 px low-passes the image and destroys scratches. The fix was to use the upscaled image only for geometry and map the polygons back to take defect crops from the original pixels.
  - Source for the cardgrade.io details: [DEV Community – cardgrade](https://dev.to/cardgrade/building-a-photo-based-card-grader-computer-vision-where-the-llm-doesnt-pick-the-score-4461)
- An academic framework measures border widths on all four edges after `cv2.getPerspectiveTransform`. — [ResearchGate: Multistage Hybrid AI Framework for Explainable Automated Trading Card Grading](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/400864149_A_Multistage_Hybrid_Artificial_Intelligence_Framework_for_Explainable_Automated_Trading_Card_Grading) (abstract/snippet only; full text 403)
- A known failure mode: when corner detection fails, auto-centering pipelines silently fall back to 50/50. In one eBay photo dataset, ~87% of raw attempts were discarded for this reason. — [GitHub issue, Mango3356/pokemon-card-grader2 #1](https://github.com/Mango3356/pokemon-card-grader2/issues/1)

### Inferences (proposed classical pipeline; algorithm design is the author's synthesis, using standard OpenCV functions)
1. **Card detection:**
   - Preprocess: grayscale or Lab L-channel, bilateral or Gaussian blur, Canny with automatic thresholds (e.g. 0.66×/1.33× the median), morphological close.
   - `cv2.findContours`, keep the largest contour with aspect ≈ 88/63 ≈ 1.397, then `approxPolyDP` to 4 points. Fall back to `minAreaRect` when there are more than 4 vertices, since rounded corners produce extra vertices.
   - More robust alternative: fit 4 lines with `HoughLinesP` or RANSAC on edge points near each side, and take the corners as the line intersections. This works despite rounded corners (Pokemon cards have ~3 mm-radius rounded corners), which make contour corner points biased inward.
2. **Homography:** `cv2.getPerspectiveTransform` / `findHomography` to a canonical canvas, e.g. 63×88 mm at 20 px/mm = 1260×1760, plus a margin. Lens distortion from phone wide-angle lenses makes straight edges bow, so either undistort or shoot from ≥30 cm with 2× zoom.
3. **Sub-pixel edge localization:**
   - Take a 1-D intensity or color-distance profile perpendicular to each side at N≈50–100 positions, spanning the full length but avoiding the ~4 mm corner radius zones.
   - Find the peak of the derivative of a Gaussian-smoothed profile, refined by parabolic (3-point) interpolation.
   - Alternatively, use the ERF/edge-spread fit used in slanted-edge MTF methods.
   - Then fit a line (least squares or RANSAC) to the sub-pixel points for both the outer cut edge and the inner frame edge. Border width as a function of position = distance between the two lines. That handles tilt (diamond cut) explicitly.
   - Report the worst ratio per axis, not the median.
4. **Ratio:** `ratio_LR = max(L,R)/(L+R)`. PSA band = smallest grade whose limit ≥ the ratio on both axes, front and back separately. Add an error margin of ±1–2 points from the calibration study.

### Gaps
- No open-source tool publishes measured accuracy against caliper or 1200 dpi ground truth for phone photos. All accuracy claims found are either unmeasured or vendor marketing.
- Whether PSA graders use a centering tool or the eye, and at which point on the card they measure, is not publicly documented.

## 3. Classical CV pipeline for corners, edges, surface and glare

### Takeaway
Classical methods exist for every factor, but none has published, validated thresholds for trading cards:
- Corner and edge whitening: color/HSV or Lab thresholding of a band just inside the cut edge, relative to an adaptively sampled border color.
- Rounding and dings: contour curvature or fitted-radius deviation.
- Scratches, print lines and dents: oriented-gradient and Gabor filters, ideally under raking light or multi-light photometric stereo.
- Glare: multi-frame registration with a soft-minimum, or cross-polarization.

The biggest limit is that a single phone photo carries only one lighting condition, which is too little for surface grading.

### Cited Findings
- **Whitening:**
  - Adaptive HSV ranges are estimated per image: split the card into a grid, sample HSV from the outer 10% border band, and score whitening as white_pixels / (blue_pixels + ε). The work describes sports or TCG backs (blue Pokemon back border).
  - HSV color masks are combined with geometric roundness to estimate corner severity.
  - Source: [Research Square: TCG-AI preprint rs-8098898](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-8098898/v1.pdf) (via search snippet; full PDF text could not be extracted)
- Vendor descriptions list Harris corner detection, radius analysis for corner sharpness, color thresholding for whitening, and Canny/Sobel/Laplacian for edges. For surface, they list line detection plus texture analysis for scratches, parallel-line clustering for print lines, blob detection plus shadow analysis for dimples, and "holographic pattern disruption" for holo clouding. They also mention a 15° raking angle for surface and photometric stereo. The same vendor claims "99.2% centering accuracy" and 89% agreement with human graders without any methodology, so treat these as marketing. — [PreGradeCards technical guide](https://pregradecards.com/blog/computer-vision-card-grading-technical-guide)
- cardgrade.io uses 16 inspection zones: 4 corners and 4 edges per side, × 2 sides. OpenCV corner metrics are fray, fill, angle, whitening and rounding. Scoring uses the weakest zone (min over corners, min over edges), with weakest-link caps.
  - Print lines are the main false positive for crease detection, because "folds change the surface normal and ink does not."
  - A crease visible on both faces (registered across sides) is stronger evidence than a wrinkle visible on one face.
  - A single phone photo is "the weakest point for surface assessment."
  - Source: [DEV Community – cardgrade](https://dev.to/cardgrade/building-a-photo-based-card-grader-computer-vision-where-the-llm-doesnt-pick-the-score-4461)
- **Photometric stereo for paper:** a 2024 JIST paper combines photometric stereo, FFT enhancement and dynamic thresholding for scratches on textured paper, tested on more than 300 samples. A related Applied Sciences (2024) paper reports 100% stable capture and 98.43% ± 1.0% recognition of scratches. — [JIST 68(3) 030503](https://library.imaging.org/jist/articles/68/3/030503); [DOAJ – Applied Sciences Sep 2024](https://doaj.org/article/e180751b3ae7486ebd024ed1be4e819a)
- **Caveat:** in photometric stereo, a change in reflectivity (e.g. printed text) can show up as false curvature, so albedo must be estimated separately. — [USPTO patent 12322128](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12322128)
- Detecting dents on metal from the inner product of photometric-stereo normals, and highlight/shadow suppression, are established in industrial inspection. — [Sensors 22(21) 8374](https://doi.org/10.3390/s22218374)
- **Glare:** Google PhotoScan registers several frames taken from slightly different angles and takes a soft-minimum per pixel, which removes glare from glossy prints. — [Google Research: PhotoScan](https://research.google/blog/photoscan-taking-glare-free-pictures-of-pictures/)
- Cross-polarization removes specular reflection, but "is not perfect." — [Cross-polarization photography guide](https://derekflood.powweb.com/renderdocsDir/photography/xpol.html)
- Collectors recommend a black, non-reflective background and diffused light placed above and in front of the card. Lighting from two directions reveals more of the holo. — [Elite Fourum: How to make photos of Pokemon cards](https://www.elitefourum.com/t/how-to-make-photos-of-pokemon-cards/36237)

### Inferences (proposed classical measurements per PSA factor; synthesis)
- **Corners:**
  - Crop ~5×5 mm at each corner of the warped image and segment the card from the background by Otsu or GrabCut.
  - Extract the contour, fit a circle to the arc (least squares), and compare its radius and residual to the factory radius from a clean reference card. Positive residual spikes indicate dings or fraying, a larger radius indicates rounding.
  - Whitening = fraction of pixels in a 0.3–1 mm band inside the contour whose Lab L* exceeds the border's reference L* + Δ, with low chroma (C* below a threshold). Use Lab/ΔE2000 rather than raw HSV, since yellow, silver and blue have different V and S.
- **Edges:**
  - Take the same thin band along each edge in 1D, at positions spaced along the side.
  - Chipping or whitening = run-lengths of low-chroma, high-L pixels.
  - Notches = deviations of the sub-pixel edge positions from the fitted line (RANSAC residuals greater than k·σ).
  - The back's dark blue border gives the highest contrast for whitening, which is why most whitening is judged on the back.
- **Surface:**
  - Inputs: a raking-light capture (phone flashlight at a low angle, several azimuths), or 4+ shots with the light moved, for photometric stereo via least-squares Lambertian normals.
  - Scratches: thin bright or dark lines in the specular image; use a Gabor bank (e.g. 8 orientations, λ ~ 4–8 px) or a Frangi ridge filter, then Hough/LSD (`cv2.createLineSegmentDetector`).
  - Print lines: long, axis-aligned, parallel lines present under diffuse light; separate them from scratches by orientation (factory roller lines run parallel to a card axis), by persistence across lighting angles, and because they don't change the normals.
  - Dents and dimples: local blobs in normal divergence or curvature, or Laplacian-of-Gaussian blobs in the shading difference between opposed light directions.
  - Creases: long ridges in the curvature map, visible on both faces.
- **Glare:**
  - Detect glare as saturated, low-saturation pixels (V≈255 with low S, or L*>95). Mask them out of all metrics, or fuse multiple angled frames with a per-pixel minimum after homography registration.
  - Holo and reverse-holo foil: run surface analysis only on the non-foil border zones, or on the diffuse/min-composite. The foil's own glitter texture triggers Gabor and scratch detectors.
- **Focus (OF) and print registration (PD):** sharpness of black outlines and text (Laplacian variance normalized to the photo's own sharpness on the card edge). Color-registration offset can be measured by cross-correlating the CMYK-like channels around text.

### Gaps
- No validated numeric thresholds (pixel counts, ΔE, radius deviation) were found that map to PSA grades for corners, edges or surface.
- No published evaluation of photometric stereo on glossy coated card stock or holofoil.
- The TCG-AI preprint's full method and accuracy could not be extracted (binary PDF).

## 4. Known open-source implementations and their accuracy

### Takeaway
There are several small hobby repos and one CC-BY measured dataset. None publishes accuracy against ground truth. The best documented approach is the Kiradex scan-line method, analyzed in detail by blakecrosley.com. The GemSnipe/rrh1441 320-photo dataset is a usable sanity-check set for centering, though biased toward gem candidates.

### Cited Findings
- **chriswrightft-create/centering-tool:**
  - Python with a Streamlit UI; modules `card_detection.py`, `border_measurement.py`, `pokemon_grader.py`.
  - Reports L/R and T/B ratios with a 45/55 pass/fail.
  - Calls itself "a heuristic model, not an official TAG-grade implementation" and warns that glare and angled photos hurt results.
  - No accuracy figures are published.
  - Source: [GitHub](https://github.com/chriswrightft-create/centering-tool)
- **rrh1441/pokemon-card-centering-measurements** (GemSnipe):
  - 320 eBay listing photos of 302 Pokemon cards, measured March–July 2026, CC-BY-4.0, published as CSV/JSON.
  - Fields: L/R and T/B left/top share, `psa10_strict_pass` (55/45), `psa9_relaxed_pass` (60/40).
  - Pipeline: corner detection, then perspective, then border measurement. Algorithms are not named.
  - No accuracy metrics. Sampling is biased (pre-selected gem candidates), with a 48.1% PSA 10-window pass rate and 89.7% PSA 9-window pass rate.
  - Source: [GitHub](https://github.com/rrh1441/pokemon-card-centering-measurements)
- **Mango3356/pokemon-card-grader2:**
  - Live phone scanner with perspective correction and auto-centering.
  - Full-art cards need manual centering correction because there is "no printed border to detect."
  - Its mapping below PSA 9 is approximate.
  - Source: [GitHub issue #1](https://github.com/Mango3356/pokemon-card-grader2/issues/1)
- **rgrizivatz/Vintage-Pokemon-Card-Centering-Detector:** limited to vintage yellow-border cards. An open issue requests modern/borderless ground truth. — [GitHub issue](https://github.com/rgrizivatz/Vintage-Pokemon-Card-Centering-Detector/issues/1)
- **NickPiscitelli/pokemon-card-analyzer:** uses TensorFlow.js plus the Canvas API, not classical OpenCV. — [GitHub](https://github.com/NickPiscitelli/pokemon-card-analyzer)
- **ce-dric/pokecard-kr #23:** a spec for OpenCV.js outer-edge and inner-frame detection with draggable correction lines. — [GitHub issue](https://github.com/ce-dric/pokecard-kr/issues/23)
- **Card Centering Calculator (hosted):** claims it detects the outside border on ~95% of cards and offers an API. This is self-reported. — [centering.joshbeetcg.com](https://centering.joshbeetcg.com/)
- **Kiradex:** the scan-line algorithm described in Section 2. Phone accuracy is unmeasured ("good to about a point" is a code comment). — [blakecrosley.com](https://blakecrosley.com/blog/how-card-centering-is-judged)
- **General OpenCV warping references** (flattening only, no border measurement): [EdjeElectronics playing card detector](https://github.com/EdjeElectronics/OpenCV-Playing-Card-Detector/blob/master/Cards.py); [alexsm OpenCV warping](https://alexsm.com/opencv-warping/)

### Inferences
- A tool that offers draggable manual-correction lines (as in pokecard-kr #23 and the pricecharting/joshbee calculators) is the practical fallback for full-art and low-contrast frames.
- Building a ground-truth set from 1200 dpi scans or calipers is necessary, because no repo provides one.

### Gaps
- No repository reports MAE in ratio points against measured ground truth.

## 5. Pokemon-specific challenges (border color, full art, holo)

### Takeaway
English cards switched from yellow to silver borders starting with Scarlet & Violet (March 31, 2023). Silver is low-chroma and close to white, which makes whitening detection on the front much harder than on yellow; the blue back remains the best whitening surface. Pokemon card borders also include design irregularities that will fool naive "first color change" detectors:
- Small print inside the bottom border from Sword & Shield onward.
- A deep e-card left strip and EX-era deep bottom borders, which are asymmetric by design.

Full-art fronts have no measurable frame, and no grader publishes how it judges them.

### Cited Findings
- **Border color change:** in December 2022 The Pokemon Company announced the yellow border would move to the silver border used in Japan, starting with the English Scarlet & Violet base set (released 2023-03-31, set code SV1EN). — [PokeBeach](https://www.pokebeach.com/?p=265577); [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2022/12/random-pokemon-trading-cards-will-no-longer-feature-iconic-yellow-border); [Pokemon.com UK](https://pokemon.com/uk/pokemon-news/pokemon-tcg-scarlet-violet-brings-changes-to-the-pokemon-trading-card-game)
- **Border widths** measured on catalog images: modern (Unbroken Bonds 2019 through Mega Evolution 2025) ~2.4 mm per side; EX and DP era 1.2–1.5 mm; Base, Fossil and Neo Genesis ~1.8–2.3 mm. — [blakecrosley.com](https://blakecrosley.com/blog/how-card-centering-is-judged)
- **Uneven by design:**
  - E-card sets (2002–03) have a deep left strip, with a median left share of ~0.75–0.77.
  - EX Ruby & Sapphire and EX Dragon have a deep bottom border (~5.1 mm bottom vs 1.5 mm top on Dragon 010).
  - From Sword & Shield on, small print sits mid-bottom-border: on Brilliant Stars 010 the bottom border drops to ~1 mm between 30% and 60% of the width, so a reader that stops at the first color change will stop there.
  - Rules should be keyed to the set, not the year.
  - Source: [blakecrosley.com](https://blakecrosley.com/blog/how-card-centering-is-judged)
- **Full art:**
  - None of six graders' public pages say how they judge front centering on frameless cards. PSA's glossary defines "full bleed" but not "full art." — [blakecrosley.com](https://blakecrosley.com/blog/how-card-centering-is-judged)
  - Guides conflict. One says graders measure to design elements such as the text box or name ([cardgrade.io](https://cardgrade.io/blog/centering-guide)); another says full-arts still have a thin frame line ([PreGradeCards phone guide](https://pregradecards.com/blog/how-to-check-centering-pokemon-cards-phone-app-2026)).
  - Back centering is always measurable, since the back has the standard border. — [PreGradeCards](https://pregradecards.com/blog/pokemon-card-centering-calculator-guide-2026)
- Holo and foil glare cannot be handled by single-shot diffuse lighting. Collectors use multiple light directions to bring out the holo. — [Elite Fourum](https://www.elitefourum.com/t/how-to-make-photos-of-pokemon-cards/36237)

### Inferences
- **Border-type classifier first.** Sample the median Lab color of the outer 1 mm band and branch:
  - yellow (b* high);
  - silver/grey (low chroma, mid-to-high L*; may be metallic, so its brightness depends on the light angle);
  - frameless (high variance, no uniform band).
  - Use set ID (from OCR of the set code or a catalog lookup) to apply per-set border templates such as the SWSH bottom-text exclusion zone and the e-card asymmetry baseline.
- **Silver borders:** the inner edge is the transition from silver to art, so use gradient magnitude in Lab or in the chroma channel rather than grayscale. Whitening on a silver front border should be scored as L*/texture anomaly against the border's own robust statistics (median ± k·MAD), not as "white pixels."
- **Yellow borders** (vintage–SWSH): whitening is high L* with chroma collapse (b* drops toward 0), which is easy to threshold.
- **Back:** the blue border (high b*-negative chroma) gives the highest whitening contrast. The inner frame is the edge of the swirl artwork, so measure back centering from the cut edge to the start of the darker swirl gradient. Its edge is soft, so use a gradient-peak fit with a larger smoothing σ.
- **Full art:** report front centering as "unmeasurable." Optionally estimate it from the position of the printed name, HP or collector-number text box relative to the cut edge, against a reference template from a well-centered catalog scan of the same card (template matching or ORB/SIFT registration to the catalog image, then the offset of the cut edge). Rely on back centering.
- **Holo and reverse holo:** foil pattern and glitter create false scratch and print-line detections. Mask foil regions, identified by high temporal variance across multi-angle frames, before surface analysis.

### Gaps
- Not verified: whether English Mega Evolution series cards (2025) keep the silver border. The blakecrosley article measures them as bordered (~2.4 mm) but does not state the color. It is likely silver, but unconfirmed.
- No source quantifies how often silver borders cause whitening false negatives or positives.
- No official PSA guidance on full-art front centering.
